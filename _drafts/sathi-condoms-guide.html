Sathi Condom Price in Pakistan (2026)

Let's get to the number everyone asks about. The sathi condom price in pakistan for a standard 12-pack currently runs about PKR 300–450, depending on the variant and where you buy. Plain and lightly lubricated versions sit at the bottom of that range; dotted or flavoured Sathi variants edge toward the top.

That's still one of the cheapest per-pack prices of any recognised brand in the country. For comparison, mid-range textured brands like Condo Max or Climax run PKR 800–1,000 for a 12-pack, and premium packs like Happy Life sit near PKR 1,500. Sathi's whole pitch is value — no fancy textures, no imported branding, just straightforward latex at a price almost anyone can afford.

A word of caution, though: if you see sathi condoms listed far below PKR 300, be suspicious. At that price point there's no margin left for legitimate stock, and repackaged or expired product is a real issue in the budget segment.

Types of Sathi Condoms You'll Find

Sathi keeps its lineup simple. The main variants you'll run into:

  • Sathi Plain/Regular — the original. Natural latex, lightly lubricated, the classic 12-pack on every pharmacy shelf.
  • Sathi Dotted — adds a textured dotted surface at a small premium over plain. The most popular upgrade within the Sathi line.
  • Sathi Flavoured — occasional flavoured editions, availability varies by city and season.

Here's the honest take: Sathi covers the basics well, but if you want serious texture, heavy lubrication, or variety, the brand's range is thin. That's exactly the gap competitors moved into. The Condo Max fruity 12-pack, for example, gives you flavoured variety Sathi simply doesn't offer, and the Dabbang ribbed 3-pack offers ribbed texture in a cheap trial size.

How to Make Sure You're Buying Original Sathi

Budget brands get faked more than premium ones, because buyers rarely scrutinise a PKR 350 pack. Check these before you buy:

  • Print quality: originals have sharp, clean printing on the foil and box. Blurry logos or off-colour packaging are red flags.
  • Expiry date: every foil should carry a legible batch and expiry date. No date, no deal.
  • Foil seal: the individual foil should feel properly sealed with a crisp edge, not flimsy or resealed.
  • Seller trust: buy from pharmacies you know or online stores with real product photos, open pricing, and cash on delivery.

When to Choose Something Other Than Sathi

I like Sathi for what it is — but there's no rule saying you have to stick with it. Here's when upgrading makes sense:

You want texture. Sathi's dotted version is mild. If you want pronounced texture, the Condo Max dotted or Climax dotted packs deliver a much more noticeable feel for a few hundred rupees more.

You want premium comfort. The Coronet Gold dotted 12-pack and Carex Classic 12-pack sit in the tier above — better lubrication, more consistent latex, and packaging that doesn't feel like a budget buy.

You want delay features. Sathi doesn't do delay condoms. The Amor Long Love delay condoms are built for exactly that, combining a dotted texture with a delay formula in a personal-care format.

You just want the basics, cheaply and reliably. Then stick with Sathi — or its closest sibling, the Condo Max regular 12-pack, which plays the same role with slightly better lubrication. For sheer luxury feel, Happy Life remains the premium reference point.
