How Timing Condoms Work

A delay condom is identical to a regular condom on the outside — natural latex, same fit, same protection. The difference is on the inside: a delay lubricant containing a mild soothing agent (benzocaine or a similar personal-care ingredient, listed on genuine packs). It works gradually over a few minutes, gently dialling down sensitivity for the wearer. Think of it as a comfort product, not a pharmaceutical — it's sold in the personal-care aisle for a reason.

Two things first-time buyers get wrong: expecting an instant effect (give it a few minutes after putting it on), and treating it as a substitute for a good fit. A loose timing condom defeats the purpose, because the lubricant only works where it makes contact. Get the fit right first; the lubricant handles the rest.

What to Check Before Buying

  • Sealed packs with readable expiry dates. Delay lubricant degrades with age and heat — old stock is the most common reason a timing condom "doesn't work."
  • The right strength. Start with a standard delay lubricant (Touch, Sathi Plus). Move to benzocaine-based options (Amor Long Love, DO Extra Delay) only if you need more.
  • Fit. Snug beats loose for delay condoms. The lubricant needs contact to work.
  • A seller that offers COD. Cash on delivery lets you inspect before paying — scammers selling fakes rarely offer it.

Storage and Usage Tips

Store timing condoms like any latex product: cool, dry, away from sunlight — never in a wallet or a hot car. The delay lubricant is the part most sensitive to heat, so a cool drawer matters more here than with classic condoms. On usage: put the condom on a few minutes before you need the effect, since the lubricant works gradually. And keep expectations grounded — these are comfort and grooming products. Most repeat buyers describe the difference as "noticeably more relaxed pacing," which is exactly what the product is designed to deliver.
