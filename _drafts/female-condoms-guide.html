What Are Female Condoms?

A female condom — also called an internal condom — is a soft, loose-fitting pouch worn inside the body instead of on the body. It's usually made of nitrile (a soft synthetic rubber) or polyurethane rather than latex, which makes it a workable option for people with latex sensitivity.

The design is simple: a flexible ring at the closed end holds it in place internally, while a larger ring at the open end stays outside the body. Like a regular condom, it's single-use — one per occasion, then disposed of. The key difference from male condoms is control: it's inserted by the wearer ahead of time, so there's no interruption at the moment itself, and it doesn't depend on the male partner for use.

Female condoms have been around internationally for decades and are a standard option in many countries' family planning programmes. In theory, they're an excellent product. In Pakistan, the reality is more complicated.

How Female Condoms Are Used

For completeness — since many searchers are simply curious — here's how they work in practice:

  • They're inserted before intimacy, similar in concept to a tampon, using the inner ring to guide and position the pouch.
  • The outer ring remains outside the body, which also helps keep the pouch from shifting during use.
  • Afterwards, the pouch is gently twisted closed and removed, then thrown away. Never reused, never flushed.
  • They shouldn't be used at the same time as a male condom — the friction between the two materials can cause both to fail.

First-time users usually need a couple of tries to get comfortable with insertion. International guides suggest practising beforehand so there's no fumbling in the moment.
