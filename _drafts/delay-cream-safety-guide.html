How Delay Creams Work (The Simple Version)

Because these are applied to sensitive skin, the safety rules are a bit stricter than for, say, a face moisturiser — and most of the "side effect" worries people search for come down to using too much, too often, or without testing first.

Step 1: Always Patch-Test First

This is the single most important habit, and it takes a day, not a minute. Apply a small amount of the cream to your inner forearm, wait 24 hours, and check for redness, itching, or irritation. No reaction? You're most likely fine to use it as directed. Any reaction at all — even mild itching — means this particular formula doesn't agree with your skin, and you should stop there rather than hoping it "settles down." This applies to every cream: MM3, Knight Rider cream, or anything else. Skin sensitivity is individual — what works for a friend may not work for you, and that's normal, not a defect in the product.

Step 2: Use the Right Amount

More cream does not mean better results — it means more chance of irritation or excessive numbness, which defeats the purpose. Start with a small, pea-sized amount and follow the packaging's own guidance. If the packaging doesn't include clear usage instructions, that's a warning sign about the product itself: genuine items from established names carry proper labelling. Give the cream time to absorb before intimacy (usually a few minutes, as directed on the pack), and wipe off any excess rather than leaving thick layers on the skin.

What to Watch For

  • Mild tingling or warmth — common with many topical personal-care products; usually passes quickly. If it doesn't, wash off.
  • Redness or itching — stop using the product. This is your skin telling you the formula isn't for you.
  • Rash or swelling — stop immediately and consult a pharmacist or doctor. Don't retry without professional advice.
  • Partner irritation — excess cream can transfer; using the right amount and allowing absorption time helps avoid this.

None of this is meant to scare you — most men use these products without any issue. But knowing the stop signals in advance is what separates careful users from careless ones.

Storage and Shelf Life

Keep creams sealed, cool, and dry — a bathroom cabinet is fine, a car dashboard in July is not. Always check the expiry date on the sealed packaging before first use, and don't use a tube past its date; old creams can irritate skin even if they were fine when fresh. If the cream's colour, smell, or texture has changed, discard it. And keep all personal-care products out of children's reach — obvious, but worth saying.
