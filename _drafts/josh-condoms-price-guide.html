Josh Delay Condoms & the Timing Question

Now the big one: josh condom timing. Search that phrase and you'll find half of Pakistan asking the same thing — which Josh is best for timing, and do timing condoms actually do anything?

Here's the plain version. Timing condoms — sold as Josh delay condoms — use slightly thicker latex, sometimes with a small amount of desensitising lubricant on the inside. The idea is simple: a little less sensation, a little more control. They don't perform miracles; they just take the edge off, and plenty of men prefer that calmer feel on its own merits.

Josh's main answer to this is the Josh Salajeet delay condom. It's the one people usually mean when they say "josh delay" — salajeet-scented, slightly thicker latex, made for longer, more relaxed sessions. It has become one of the most talked-about Josh condoms timing options online, mostly because it delivers the thicker-latex feel without the cheap, plasticky texture some budget delay condoms have.

Some men pair a timing condom with a personal care routine beforehand. If that's your style, combining a Josh delay condom with something like Knight Rider Delay Spray is a common combo — the condom handles the latex side while the spray is part of the pre-use ritual. Others prefer the all-in-one simplicity of Vimax timing spray alongside their regular condom. There's no single right answer; it's about what feels comfortable and natural to you.

A few practical notes on josh condoms timing that actually matter more than the variant you pick: roll it on correctly (rim down, air squeezed out of the tip), add extra water-based lubricant if either of you feels friction — dryness is the real enemy of comfort — and never double up. Two condoms rub against each other and that friction makes breakage more likely, not less. One good condom, fitted right, beats two every single time.

Josh Classic: The Everyday Workhorse

Josh Classic is the plain, no-nonsense option — natural latex, standard lubrication, no flavours, no textures. It's the condom equivalent of a white t-shirt: not exciting, but it goes with everything. If you're new to condoms, or you're buying for reliability rather than novelty, Classic is the sensible starting point. It's also the cheapest way to figure out your size and fit preferences before you spend more on the fancy variants.

Honestly, a lot of experienced buyers keep coming back to Classic even after trying the whole range. Flavours are fun, textures are interesting, but for regular use the plain version wins on comfort and price. Nothing wrong with boring when boring works.

Josh Ultra Thin: For a Closer Feel

On the opposite end from the delay line sits the Josh Ultra Thin. Thinner latex, marketed for a more natural, sensitive feel. Some men swear the difference is night and day; others barely notice. If the standard Josh feels a bit "there," this is the one to try next.

Quick Tips for First-Time Buyers

  • Storage matters: heat damages latex. Don't keep condoms in a wallet for weeks or on a sunny dashboard — a cool drawer is perfect.
  • Check the date: every foil has an expiry printed on it. Old condoms lose elasticity.
  • Lube helps everything: a little extra water-based lubricant improves comfort for both partners, whatever the variant.
  • One is enough: never stack two condoms. Said it before, saying it again — it matters.
  • Try before you stock up: grab 3-packs of a few types (say, Sindhri mango and Classic) before committing to a 36-pack of anything.
